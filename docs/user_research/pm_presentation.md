## 🎯 Slide 3: Discovered Barriers (Thematic Affinity Map)
* **Slide Header**: Thematic Affinity Map: Why Customers Stay Isolated in Habits
* **Visual Layout**:
  * A 3-column structured matrix grouping our 5 core affinity mapping themes:
    1. **Pricing Overhead**: Delivery and handling fees on small add-on purchases.
    2. **Trust & Quality Deficit**: Rotten fresh produce; lack of easy cash refunds.
    3. **Habit Isolation**: Homepage clutter forces users to rely strictly on reordering order histories.
    4. **Information Gaps**: Lack of size charts, ingredient lists, reviews, and warranty details.
    5. **Inventory Instability**: Unstable stock levels and unconsented product substitutions.
* **Script**:
  > "By mapping observations from our deep-dive user interviews, we identified five clear friction pillars. First, delivery fees on small orders act as a massive tax, stopping users from adding simple toiletries to their daily milk orders. Second, a single bad experience with squished tomatoes ruins fresh produce trust permanently, especially since we refund to app wallets rather than cash. Third, the app's home screen is visually cluttered, forcing senior citizens and convenience seekers to bypass discovery entirely and retreat into their order history. Fourth, for high-consideration purchases like diapers or kitchen appliances, our app lacks sizing charts, warranty details, and reviews—meaning users default back to Amazon or Nykaa. And finally, inventory instability and unconsented substitutions frustrate gourmet planners, driving them straight to Zepto or Blinkit."

  * **HMW 3**: *How might we simplify the homepage navigation to make discovery stress-free?*
    * *Quote*: "The homepage is very confusing with moving banners... it feels like a busy railway station." - Vijay
* **Script**:
  > "Based on our validated findings, we have defined three core problem statements to guide our product roadmap. First, we need to solve the delivery fee tax on cross-category add-ons. Second, we must address the freshness trust deficit. If local vendors can offer easy physical returns and trust, quick commerce must match it. And third, we need to simplify homepage navigation to prevent users from hiding inside their historical reorder loops."
