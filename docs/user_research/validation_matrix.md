# Part 2: Customer-Backed Problem Statements

Based on the validated research, we have formulated three core **How Might We (HMW)** problem statements to guide our product roadmap:

### 1. Delivery Fee Barrier on Cross-Category Shopping
* **Problem Statement**: Users who rely on Instamart for low-value daily utility items (e.g. milk, bread) are blocked from buying auxiliary categories (e.g. personal care, household essentials) because the additional items do not meet the free-delivery threshold, leading to high cart abandonment.
* **How Might We**: *How might we allow users to add low-consideration auxiliary items to their daily essential carts without triggering additional delivery fees?*
* **Supporting Evidence (Quotes)**:
  * Rohan: *"Why would I buy a face wash here when I have to pay 35 rupees delivery fee just for a single item?"*
  * Priya: *"I forgot to add dishwashing liquid. Now I have to order again and pay 30 rupees delivery fee. Give me a 2-minute window to append items."*

### 2. The Freshness & Trust Deficit
* **Problem Statement**: Users refuse to purchase fresh vegetables, fruits, and meats on Instamart because they have experienced poor quality control and find the app's catalog descriptions lacking in trust parameters (certification, packaging dates, real-life pictures).
* **How Might We**: *How might we build absolute trust in the freshness and quality of Instamart's fresh produce category so that users confidently transition from local markets to our app?*
* **Supporting Evidence (Quotes)**:
  * Rohan: *"Two of the tomatoes were completely squished and moldy... Now, I don't trust them with fresh items."*
  * Priya: *"I need to know if the grapes are organic and wash-safe... there is no certification label, no farm details."*
  * Meera: *"If the local vendor sells me bad potatoes, he replaces them without question. On the app... they only offer a refund to the app wallet."*

### 3. Homepage Clutter & Habit Isolation
* **Problem Statement**: The density of promotional banners, animated carousels, and surge alerts on the homepage creates visual overwhelm, forcing non-tech-savvy and habit-driven users to bypass the homepage entirely and isolate themselves inside their reorder histories.
* **How Might We**: *How might we simplify the homepage navigation and search experience to make product discovery stress-free and accessible for non-tech-savvy users?*
* **Supporting Evidence (Quotes)**:
  * Vijay: *"The homepage is very confusing with moving banners... it feels like a busy railway station. I get anxious... I just want a simple list of my regular items."*
  * Rohan: *"The banner ads at the top look like spam, I always scroll down straight to my history."*
